00INTO STORYMODE

BodyPrompt
Prompting as Choreographic Search

A practice-based research project on poetic language, AI motion generation, and choreographic selection — presented as a lecture performance.
William Wong
01OVERVIEW

What is BodyPrompt, and what did we build?

  • A practice built on prompting as choreographic search — using poetic, expressive language as an iterative prompt into AI motion-generation models. not a new motion system — a new way of thinking about human–AI collaborative search
  • Outputs are visualised as stick-figure notation — the raw computational substrate, shown honestly rather than dressed up.
  • A selection loop closes the system: I take the role of choreographer — observing the generated score and choosing, rather than performing it myself.
02OVERVIEW

Where this came from

  • An immersive music experience — sound and space as a prompt for movement, not instruction. the seed of treating a prompt as inspiration rather than a command
  • A short poem of my own — the first material I used to test whether language could choreograph.
  • The choreographer/dancer relationship as a model: a choreographer inspires a dancer to move — they don't necessarily instruct. this became the guiding metaphor for how I wanted to prompt a machine
03OVERVIEW

The research question

If a choreographer can move a dancer through image and metaphor rather than instruction — can a poem move a machine the same way?

What happens between a poetic prompt and a generated body — and what does the machine's misunderstanding tell us about movement itself?
04METHOD

Preparing the system

  • Basic prompt → body-movement pipeline: text in, joint motion out. shared exchange format across models: BVH / canonical joint-JSON
  • Selected multiple motion-generation models for comparison rather than committing to one.
  • Before any testing: set an expected result, so the comparison could surprise us.

06PROCESS

Starting the experiment

  • Calibration — confirm every system behaves as expected before testing anything poetic.
  • Head-first: examine the quality of generated movement using poetic prompts directly. what does a model do with language it wasn't built for?
  • Introduced graded prompt levels to control how literal the language became.
07PROCESS

Prompt levels

01Originalthe poem line, untouched
02Semanticmeaning extracted from the line
03Body Qualityqualities of movement implied
04Action Descriptionnamed physical actions
05Explicit Instructionliteral, model-ready command
A rating system was implemented to identify which level produced the best movement.
08METHOD · EXAMPLE

One cue, five levels

Source: Pina Bausch, Wiesenland (2000) — cue "Trance"
OriginalTrance
SemanticA person is in a trance, absorbed in an altered state of awareness and only faintly responsive to the world around them.
Body QualityThe body feels absorbed and inward, with a distant focus, suspended attention, softened responsiveness, and an uncanny sense of being drawn elsewhere.
Action DescriptionThe person moves slowly with a distant gaze, pauses unpredictably, sways gently, and lets the arms drift with delayed reactions.
Explicit InstructionStand upright with an unfocused gaze. Slowly sway the torso from side to side, pause, take two slow steps forward, let both arms drift upward, then gradually lower them while remaining distant and unresponsive.

10PROCESS · PART 2

Creating our poem

  • Current models are trained to follow clear instruction — the opposite of how a choreographer actually speaks to a dancer. how do we introduce a real choreographer/dancer relationship into the system?
  • Wrote my own poem, head-first, and examined what it produced.
  • Used an LLM to suggest variations of action-described prompts — the AI's own improvisation on my line. each variation generated once only — no cherry-picking through repetition
11PROCESS · PART 2

Human in the loop, as a game

  • A second system lets a human choose between the generated movements — turning selection itself into the choreographic act.
  • Each poem line offers three body-prompt interpretations, each paired with a pre-generated movement.
  • Chosen movements assemble into one continuous performance, captioned by the poem. this became "Searching Home" / The Other One
12FUTURE WORK

Where this goes next

  • Fold the LLM-suggestion and selection process directly into the BodyPrompt system.
  • Open more interfaces to connect new AI models as they appear.
  • Work directly with a choreographer and dancer to explore the practice beyond my own body.
