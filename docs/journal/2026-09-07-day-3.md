# Day 3 — nothing generated, 315 judged

## 1. Where I started

Day 2 produced 1,215 motions and ended with two debts, neither of them about models.

The first: **nobody outside my own machine could check any of it.** The corpus is 870 MB in
five directories that are not in the repository, and the entry quoted from it without
publishing what it quoted. The second, which the entry named itself: *I can generate 1,215
motions in an afternoon and have no way to record what I think of any of them — my judgement
lives in my head and in whatever I remember by evening, which is not evidence.*

Today put no prompts to any model. The workers stayed down and no new movement exists. What
changed is that yesterday's corpus became citable, and — for the first of its four
practices — judged. **This is the first day of the project that produced a result rather than
a dataset.**

## 2. Publishing the corpus without publishing the corpus

Day 2 cannot ship its session files the way Day 1 did — 870 MB against 25 MB — so the
practice of *"import them and look at the same bodies"* does not hold for it. Rather than
quietly drop the promise I stated the exception and worked out what could stand in its place.

**Every prompt, verbatim**: all 135 Stage 2 prompts — 27 cues at five ladder levels — plus
the 23 calibration prompts. The part that matters is how it was made: **not by hand.** Every
string is read out of `raw.jsonl`, the record of what was actually sent to each worker, and
checked byte for byte against the prompts parsed from the original briefs. The generator
refuses to emit a document if the two disagree, if a prompt was run that is not in a brief,
or if the nine runs behind one prompt carry different text.

Writing it caught me making a category error. My first pass flattened a per-model fact into a
per-prompt one: the nine runs behind DH1 disagreed, not on the prompt but on
`facialDataNote`. SnapMoGen and Kimodo have no facial channel at all; Language of Motion
reports `parts_generated=['upper','lower']`. That is a difference between models, and the
check caught me writing it down as the wrong kind of thing.

## 3. The other half of the comparison

**The documented human responses**, separately: 27 cues, **32 response records**, 12 sources,
each with a URL and an evidence label — `direct video`, `rehearsal account`, `performer
account`, `review`, `motion-tracking study`, `museum documentation`, `insufficient detail`,
`source unconfirmed`.

The counts differ because five of Hay's records are a second or third performer answering a
score another performer had already answered, and the tracking study behind DH5 records
*seven takes each* from three performers. **The same score does not have one bodily answer**,
and it does not have one even from the same body on the same afternoon. Any framework
treating the documented human response as a target the model approaches has to say which of
Durning's seven takes is the target, and there is no principled answer.

Two things came out of labelling the evidence that I had not seen while collecting it.

**Only 5 of 32 records are video, and all five are Gaga** — Naharin demonstrating a term, not
a dancer answering a cue. Nothing in the Bausch, Hay or Forti material is footage of the
response itself. **This is a corpus of *descriptions* of movement**, and that is a ceiling on
every comparison built from it.

**SF6, *See Saw*, has no confirmed source.** Every other cue gained a citation; this one lost
one. It is marked `source unconfirmed` and excluded from comparison rather than left to
borrow the citation of the work beside it in the table.

And **DH2 and DH3 describe decision-making, not reproducible movement.** Their sources
establish how each performer *chose*, not what her body did. A generated motion cannot be
compared against a process.

## 4. The rating instrument

Then the tool Day 2 asked for. Press <kbd>A</kbd> and the poem rail becomes a rating list.

**One axis, deliberately.** How much of the cue is perceptible in the body that answered it —
not whether the movement is good, and not whether it is complex. Day 2 had already found that
visible complexity is not the test. Five steps with written anchors, on screen while in use,
because a scale whose wording drifts between sittings is measuring the reader rather than the
movement.

Three distinctions the format refuses to blur, each a claim about the research rather than
about software:

- **`unrated` is not `0`.** Nobody has judged this, versus someone judged it and found
  nothing there. Collapsing them would let an unwatched motion count as evidence of absence.
- **`skip` is not a low score.** It is the answer for a motion I cannot judge, and keeping it
  out of the numbers stops *"I could not tell"* being counted as *"there was nothing there"*.
- **A judgement belongs to the movement it was given to**, not to the line.

Rating a line invalidates nothing. Looking at a movement is not editing the score.

## 5. What building it exposed

**Import was discarding the record that makes a judgement mean anything.** The Day 2 stage
files carry the cue, prompt level, seed and model per line; the importer rebuilt each line
from a fixed list of known fields, so all of it was dropped in silence. I could have rated
315 motions and been unable to say which rung of the ladder any of them came from.

**The stage was rebuilding the whole corpus on every keypress** — about 25,200 frames at 105
lines. **And the browser's copy was paying for the whole corpus to save one integer**: 828 ms
against 0.5 ms with the motions lifted out. Both are the cost of using the instrument for
something it had not been used for, at the size a real corpus reaches.

## 6. The first ratings — Stage 2A, Pina Bausch

**315 judgements: 7 cues × 5 levels × 3 seeds × 3 models.** A complete census, not a sample.
Nothing unrated, and `skip` was never used once.

| level | mean | rated ≥3 |
|---|---|---|
| **O** original | 0.73 | 5% |
| **S** semantic | 0.73 | 10% |
| **Q** body quality | 1.06 | 11% |
| **A** action description | **2.49** | **62%** |
| **E** explicit instruction | **2.63** | **65%** |

**There is a threshold, and it is not where the ladder assumed.** The step from Q to A is
**1.43 on a 0–4 scale**. The mean spread across the three seeds of the same prompt is
**0.34**, and 73 of 105 seed-triples are identical — so the level effect is four times the
seed noise, and nothing here is an accident of sampling.

**The ladder has two rungs, not five.** O, S and Q are not meaningfully different from one
another in outcome; A and E are not meaningfully different from each other. Everything
happens in one place: where the prompt begins naming observable actions.

This does not overturn Day 2's finding that there is no point at which movement becomes
*dance*. That was a different axis. This is about where a cue becomes **legible**, which is
what the scale was built to measure, and on that axis the discontinuity is sharp.

## 7. Three models, three relationships with specificity

Pooling hides the most interesting result. Across the bottom of the ladder the models move in
**opposite directions**.

| model | O | S | Q | A | E | mean | ≥3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kimodo | 1.10 | 0.76 | **0.62** | 3.62 | 3.29 | 1.88 | 44% |
| SnapMoGen | 1.10 | 1.29 | 2.05 | 3.24 | **3.90** | 2.31 | 48% |
| Language of Motion | 0.00 | 0.14 | 0.52 | 0.62 | 0.71 | 0.40 | **0%** |

**Kimodo goes backwards at Q.** Body-quality language — *"the body feels absorbed and inward,
with a distant focus, suspended attention"* — reads **worse in the body than the bare cue
"Trance"**. Nothing at that rung reaches 3. Then action language recovers it to 3.62. It is
the one place the ladder runs in reverse, and it is a specific claim about what Kimodo can
read: a described *state* is not something it can act on, while the poetic original at least
gives it a word to retrieve against.

**SnapMoGen is the only monotonic climber**, the only model that gets anything from Q, and
the only one peaking at E rather than A. Where the ladder's premise holds, it holds here.

**Language of Motion did not participate.** Mean 0.40, ceiling of 2, and **0 of 105 rated
≥3** — its curve nearly flat, so explicit instruction barely helps it either. Day 1 observed
from three prompts that it could not distinguish a word from a five-sentence instruction.
This is that observation with 105 judgements behind it.

## 8. What a high score at A actually means

The result I most distrust is the one that looks best.

Day 2 recorded that the translation up the ladder is **mine** — no model rewrites anything,
`promptRewriteApplied` is false on all 1,215 motions. So by level A the interpretive work has
already been done by me. *"The person reaches outward toward an imagined other person, draws
the hands back toward the body"* is not a cue that survived into a body; it is an instruction
that was followed.

Which means the ladder may cross between two different questions rather than measuring one
continuum: at **O–Q** the score is about whether a model can *interpret*, and at **A–E** it is
largely about whether it can *execute*. Eight of 21 cue-model pairs went from ≤1 at O to ≥3 at
A or E, and none went the other way — but *"the ladder rescued the cue"* and *"the ladder
replaced the cue with something else"* produce exactly the same number.

I do not think that makes the 1.43 step uninteresting. It makes it a finding about **what
kind of language these systems can act on**, which is a smaller and more defensible claim
than the one the ladder was built to test.

## 9. What re-rating measured

I re-rated Kimodo's Q cell the next morning, after all 315 had been judged. Of 21 motions, 8
changed — **every one upward**, mean +0.43, 62% exact agreement and 95% within one point.

That is the Day 3 question — *do the anchors hold their meaning across several hundred
judgements* — answered with a number, and the answer is **a qualified no**. They hold to
within a point, but they drift, and my early judgements were harsher: the original Q was the
fourth file I ever rated, before I had seen the range the corpus covers.

The dip survives it. Q is still Kimodo's worst rung, still below the bare cue, and still
reaches 3 on nothing.

**One caution belongs on the record.** I re-rated the cell that produced the surprising
result, and only that one. Re-checking anomalies and keeping the revised value shrinks
anomalies whether or not they were wrong. Separating scale drift from having been told this
cell looked extreme needs a re-rate of an *unremarkable* cell under the same conditions, and
that has not been done. Both files are kept; the first pass is not overwritten.

## 10. What I am not claiming

Three practices are unrated — Hay, Naharin and Forti, 900 motions. Bausch's cues are the most
abstract of the four, and whether the Q→A step is a general property or a Bausch fact is
exactly what the other three would show.

**The rating was not blind.** I judged file by file, one level at a time, always in
O→S→Q→A→E order. A staircase is what expectation would produce. What argues against pure
order effect is that the three model blocks were rated at different times and produced three
*different* shapes — a dip, a flat line, a climb — where drift alone would repeat one shape.
That is suggestive, not conclusive, and a blind shuffled re-rate would settle it.

One rater. No inter-rater reliability. And the scale is being used more like *no / weak /
yes* than as five steps: 35% zeros, 23% ones, then only 11% twos.

## 11. Where this leaves the research

The corpus is citable, judgeable, and for one practice, judged. The first real finding is
narrower than the question that produced it and I would rather state it small: **these three
models diverge most where the language is least explicit, and converge where it is most.**

What is owed, in the order it would change things:

- **A blind, shuffled re-rate** of a subset spanning all levels and models. The single
  highest-value hour available, and it needs a shuffle mode the instrument does not have.
- **A control re-rate of an unremarkable cell**, to separate scale drift from knowing a result.
- **Stages 2B, 2C and 2D** — 900 motions, and the test of whether any of this is general.
- **A source for SF6**, or a decision to exclude *See Saw*.
