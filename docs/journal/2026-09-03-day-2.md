# Day 2 — From poetic cue to movement instruction

## 1. Where I started today

Day 1 asked whether text-to-motion AI can generate dance or only body movement, and left me a
sharper problem: the models retrieve where a dancer associates. Today's question is about
degree rather than kind.

**How descriptive does a choreographic prompt need to become before different text-to-motion
models produce a recognisable bodily response, and what is lost when poetic ambiguity is
progressively translated into explicit movement instruction?**

I want to stay careful about what I think these systems are. Not a collective human mind — a
statistical archive of associations between language, bodies, movement descriptions and
cultural representations of action that happened to get written down.

## 2. Three models, three relationships with language

**SnapMoGen / MoMask++ — interpretation.** In its published design, expressive language may
first be rewritten into detailed physical description before generation. That rewriting is
what I most wanted to watch. It does not happen in my build: no rewrite stage exists in the
worker, so the model prompt equals the user prompt on every motion today. The role is real in
the architecture and absent from my data.

**Kimodo — composition.** Strongest as language becomes physically and spatially explicit.
Text-only generation throughout, to keep it comparable.

**Language of Motion — embodied language.** Relevant to gesture, body parts and non-verbal
communication. Restricted here to text-to-motion.

These differences are the experiment, not noise to remove. Three systems that read language
differently are three instruments, and their disagreement is the measurement.

## 3. Stage 1 calibration

Before any choreographic material I calibrated each system with prompts from its own published
examples — the kind of sentence each was built to answer. Kimodo first, then the other two.
Twenty-three motions.

Not artistic judgement: confirmation that BodyPrompt is talking to each model correctly —
right prompt in, valid motion out, durations honoured, seeds recorded. For SnapMoGen I kept
both the user and model prompts. They are identical, and the record says so.

## 4. Stage 2 prompt ladder

Each cue was translated through five levels:

- **O — Original**
- **S — Semantic**
- **Q — Body quality**
- **A — Action description**
- **E — Explicit movement instruction**

Poetic / indirect → semantic meaning → bodily quality → observable action → explicit
instruction.

The point is not to show that explicit instructions work better; I assume they will. The
question is **where on the ladder each model starts producing a recognisable bodily response,
and what has been discarded by the time it does.** Whatever a model needs before it answers is
a description of what it cannot hear.

## 5. Experimental controls

Three variations per prompt; seeds 42, 1234 and 5678, all supported. The same user-facing text
to all three models. Eight seconds throughout. No manual selection, no regeneration because a
result looked strange, no model-specific improvement, no Kimodo spatial constraints.

Most importantly, **the documented dancer response is withheld from generation entirely** —
it reaches the models through no channel. If I let a historical solution into a descriptive
prompt and then found the AI producing it, I would have discovered nothing but my own
contamination. The human response is comparison evidence, never ground truth.

## 6. Four choreographic practices

**Pina Bausch** — situation, emotion, association: *Trance*, *Something with strength and
energy*, *How would you like others to treat you?*, *Trevi Fountain*, *Sharp turn*,
*Desperate longing*. Almost no explicit movement information in any.

**Deborah Hay** — attention, perception, paradox: joy and sorrow crossing the face without
resolving; a wordless song generating movement; an unfamiliar stride along a curved path;
travelling in a straight line while erasing the destination. Some describe attention rather
than visible movement. The joy/sorrow cue is a deliberate representational stress test:
SMPL-22 cannot encode facial expression, and none of the three models generated face data.

**Ohad Naharin / Gaga** — somatic images: Lena, Biba, Oba, Ashi, Tashi, bones floating inside
flesh, both arms as one long rope, a quake cultivated in the pelvis. These ask for something
imagined or internally sensed rather than executed.

**Simone Forti** — task and rule: Huddle, Slant Board, Hangers, Roller Boxes, Platforms, See
Saw, Censor. I postponed Forsythe, whose most relevant prompts lean on visible geometric cues
while this phase is about language as the primary input. Forti's difficulty is different: the
text can be entirely concrete while the model lacks the world it needs — another body, a rope,
a board, shared weight, contact.

## 7. An emerging second axis

I began with one axis: **poetic ambiguity ←→ explicit bodily instruction**.

Forti suggests a second: **individual / internal body ←→ relational / material world**.

A Gaga cue can be impossible to see and still perfectly possible for one body. A Forti score
can be plainly describable and still unperformable, because the apparatus and the other people
are missing. Different failures, which I had been treating as one. Not a taxonomy yet — a
space to test against the data.

## 8. Dataset created

| practice | motions |
|---|---|
| Pina Bausch | 315 |
| Deborah Hay | 225 |
| Ohad Naharin / Gaga | 360 |
| Simone Forti | 315 |
| **total** | **1,215** |

Plus 23 calibration motions.

I expected the corpus to hold textual transformations as well as motions, so the evidence
would run *choreographic cue → prompt translation → model interpretation → generated
movement*. It does — but the translation step is **mine**. The ladder is my writing. The
model-side rewriting I hoped to observe is absent, and that absence is itself a result.

## 9. Leave the results open

I have not looked at what the bodies did, and deliberately will not today. What I carry
forward:

At which prompt level does each model begin producing recognisable movement? What is lost as
language becomes more machine-legible? Does more description reduce interpretive diversity?
Which cues collapse into familiar gestures? What would SnapMoGen preserve, remove or invent if
it rewrote? How much varies across three seeds? When is failure a matter of language
ambiguity, and when of missing relational structure? And how do these responses sit beside the
documented human ones, without treating the human as correct?

Day 2 established the experimental instrument. Analysis of what the generated bodies actually
did remains open.
